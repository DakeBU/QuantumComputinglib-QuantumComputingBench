How much fidelity can a tree reach before the layer budget runs out?

Assumptions

  • Let TT be a finite nonempty tree rooted at rr. Each vertex vv labels a real coordinate and has a known signed target amplitude ava_v, with ∑vav2=1\sum_v a_v^2=1. The input is ere_r, the positive unit coordinate at the root.
  • One layer chooses a matching of tree edges. On an edge ordered as (parent, child) it may apply any block (c−ssc)\begin{pmatrix}c&-s\\ s&c\end{pmatrix}, with real c,sc,s satisfying c2+s2=1c^2+s^2=1. Vertices outside the selected edges keep their amplitudes. There are at most d≥0d\ge0 layers, where dd is an integer.

Conventions

  • Disjointness refers to vertices in the same layer. A vertex may participate again in a later layer. Empty layers and arbitrary choices of exact angles are allowed.
  • The score of an output yy is its squared fidelity with aa, namely |∑vavyv|2|\sum_v a_vy_v|^2. A common sign is immaterial to the score. For a vertex vv, TvT_v consists of vv and all its descendants.

Question

Determine the largest possible score for arbitrary T,a,dT,a,d. Give a recurrence with a table indexed by a vertex vv and a remaining layer budget 0≤t≤d0\le t\le d. Explain how to evaluate it in time polynomial in |V(T)||V(T)| and d+1d+1, counting real arithmetic and comparisons as unit operations. You may use polynomial-time maximum-weight matching in a bipartite graph.

Prove an upper bound for every circuit, including circuits that revisit an edge or cancel an amplitude. Then show how to choose a circuit attaining your answer for signed targets. Address zero target mass among the sites reached by a schedule, a zero budget, and a tree consisting only of the root.

For a five-vertex example, the root rr has children u,vu,v, with respective single children u′,v′u',v'. The only nonzero target entries are au′=1/2a_{u'}=1/\sqrt2 and av′=−1/2a_{v'}=-1/\sqrt2. Compute the optimum at d=1,2,3d=1,2,3 and use it to assess the claim that all target mass within graph distance dd can always be captured by depth dd.
