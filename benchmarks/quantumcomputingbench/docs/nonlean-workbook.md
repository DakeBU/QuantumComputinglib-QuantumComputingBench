# Supplementary natural-language mathematics workbook

The first November intake joined two overlapping collaborator deliveries into a
cohort of twelve mathematical packets. The continuation archive contains the
original twelve packets and thirteen additional contribution files.

Batch 2 adds six public study candidates across Gram contractions, phase
suppliers and error composition. The geometry intake adds six more in two
chapters. The combined workbook has 24 packets in eight chapters, with
provisional construction bands D2 (five), D3 (nine) and D4 (ten). D0 is the
knowledge facet within each exercise. Readers can use the workbook without
a Lean installation.

## Identity and evaluation scope

Four packets provide additional expositions of existing SP006, LA015, SP007 and
SP114 contracts. Their original families, test/dev splits and mathematical
identities remain unchanged. The new exposition does not replace current gold.
Twenty expanded targets remain candidates with unassigned splits. In particular,
the quantitative coherent-family phase problem is broader than an ordinary
same-contract view of LA016 or BE104; its sign example and phase foundations
already overlap existing material.

The workbook adds no canonical core, Lean admission or canonical train/evaluation
row. Its question-only JSONL, references, review index and node reverse index
are separate supplementary downloads. Shared normalization, rotation-distance
and phase ingredients must be reconciled before any future scored pooling.
Public problems and solutions are not a hidden holdout.

## Local rotations and phase cycles

The local rotation chapter progresses from identifying a gate by cut masses
to proving a sharp preparation cost on a line and optimizing fidelity under
a tree layer budget. The phase cycle chapter moves from diagonal transport
through a permutation to pinned phase calibration and recovery from power
traces. The new claims have reviewed natural-language derivations. Their
Lean graph links point to the underlying definitions and results.

Each exercise keeps its original question, weighted rubric, worked reference
and source anchors. The two chapters provide six printable question sheets.
Use the question-only JSONL for respondent inputs and the separate reference
and graph downloads for grading and review. The [intake record](../reports/nonlean-intake-20261114/intake.json)
documents source scope and shared reasoning with nearby public questions.

For an offline practice run, select `rotation` or `cycles` in `index.json`.
Take only the matching rows from `questions.jsonl` into the respondent
workspace. Join `references.jsonl` in the grader workspace on both `view_id`
and `question_sha256`. Use the ten-point rubric to record criterion-level
credit. Keep related variants in the same exposure family when designing
future splits. The current downloads support practice and rubric development.

Sealed intake review artifacts have an explicit `.gitattributes` exception that
preserves raw bytes and original line endings. Source/data files retain the
repository's LF policy. No line-ending normalization silently re-seals an
independent derivation or transfers an old approval.

## Reproduce the intake checks

```sh
python -m unittest website.scripts.test_qcb_nonlean_workbook -v
python benchmarks/quantumcomputingbench/scripts/curriculum.py validate
```

The tests check duplicate accounting, split boundaries, source/phase/notation
bindings, question/reference separation, exact download projections, original
question preservation and scope ancestry. Browser gates compile actual formulas,
open solutions/rubrics/maps by keyboard, check graph links and test mobile and
desktop layouts in all three website themes. Commit-pinned provenance links
must resolve to actual blobs in the publication commit before publishing.
