A one-qubit example

Work in \(\mathbb C^2\) with ordered basis \(|0\rangle,|1\rangle\). Here the preparation contract is a unitary matrix that takes the first basis vector to a specified unit vector. To prepare \(|+\rangle=(|0\rangle+|1\rangle)/\sqrt2\), use the Hadamard matrix.

\[ H=\frac1{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}, \qquad H|0\rangle=\frac1{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix}1\\1\end{pmatrix}. \]
  1. Each column has squared norm \((1+1)/2=1\), and their inner product is \((1-1)/2=0\). Thus \(H^\dagger H=I_2\), where \(H^\dagger\) is the conjugate transpose and \(I_2\) is the \(2\times2\) identity. Since \(H\) is square, this proves it is unitary.
  2. Multiplication by \(|0\rangle=(1,0)^T\) selects the first column, which is the requested state.

The identity gives the final amplitudes. By the Born rule, measuring in the specified basis gives probabilities \(1/2\) and \(1/2\). The state \((|0\rangle-|1\rangle)/\sqrt2\) gives the same probabilities in this basis, so these probabilities leave the relative phase undetermined.
