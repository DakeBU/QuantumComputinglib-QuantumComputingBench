Licenses and reuse

Reuse with the right license and attribution

The project's default license is MIT. Third-party components keep their own terms. Supplying Apache 2.0 text does not make this an Apache-licensed repository.

What can I reuse?

MaterialScope and terms
Project-authored code, Lean proofs, documentation, website templates and original diagrams MIT by default, unless a file or component states otherwise. Retain the copyright and permission notice.
Vendored MathJax 3.2.2Apache-2.0. Keep its bundled license and existing provenance/modification note; do not relabel it MIT.
Other third-party code, adapted material and dependency runtimesTheir own licenses and notices continue to apply. Check the exact upstream component and version.
External papers, textbook figures, datasets and Lean referencesA citation, technical card, graph node or benchmark reference is not permission to copy, redistribute or train on the source. Check its terms separately.

Independently authored local proofs are distinct from copied upstream material. Inclusion in the Lean Graph does not change a license, certify a proof or imply endorsement.

When a component uses Apache 2.0

Include the license, retain applicable copyright, patent, trademark and attribution notices, prominently identify modified files, and carry forward relevant upstream NOTICE content when supplied. The license includes patent conditions and does not grant general trademark rights. This is a reading aid, not a change to the license.

Our project notices and bibliography do not substitute for an upstream NOTICE when redistributing that component. Do not assume MIT and Apache are interchangeable.

Attribution and documentation design

Sho Sonoda's Lean Ridgelet inspired the organization of mathematical exposition linked to Lean declarations. Verso and Verso Blueprint provide the upstream Blueprint renderer and styles. Inspiration, dependency use and copied material are different kinds of reuse; each retains its appropriate credit and terms.
